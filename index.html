Introduction

Nobody voted for this.

Nobody ever voted to slow the company down.

There was no meeting where it was decided that customers should wait longer, or that capable people should spend Thursday preparing for Friday. It accumulated, the way lint does.

I’ve spent twenty years in analytics, close to the place where a company’s data meets its decisions. What I kept finding is that organizations rarely fail for lack of information or good intentions. They have more data than they can turn into insight and more strategy than they can turn into action. They fail in the gap between knowing and doing.

Here is how it happens.

Every one of those was a good idea on the day it was made. That’s what makes it hard to see. There is no bad decision to point at. There’s only a customer who used to wait a day and now waits nine, and a company that is very busy explaining why.

The hardest thing to see is what everyone has learned to accept.

What this book is

This is a book about that line. How it gets crowded, why nobody notices, and what it takes to clear it and keep it clear.

It isn’t a method. There is nothing to fill in and nothing to score. It’s a way of looking.

It looks in three places. How the business creates value. How the system creates leverage. How people get to do their best work. Each shows what the others miss, and the problems that matter most tend to live where they overlap.

Underneath are seven forces. They read like nouns. They behave like verbs.

  1. 01Awareness. Seeing what is actually happening.
  2. 02Trust. Making it safe to say so.
  3. 03Conditions. Making good work possible.
  4. 04Value. Keeping the customer in the room.
  5. 05Incentives. Rewarding what you say you want.
  6. 06Leverage. Making an improvement last without a hero.
  7. 07Excellence. Holding the standard when nobody is checking.

They compound in whichever direction they point. When they reinforce one another, individual talent becomes collective performance. When they work against one another, dysfunction compounds just as reliably.

Culture is what everyday decisions and consequences reinforce, not what is written on the walls.

How to read it

Slowly, if you can. The chapters are short, and each one ends with something to ponder. It isn’t a test, and there is no answer to send anywhere. It’s something to notice the next time you’re at work.

You won’t meet anyone in these pages who is doing it wrong on purpose. Everyone here is reasonable. That is the problem. It’s also the good news, because reasonable people can change a system once they can see it.

Something to ponder

Somewhere in your week there is a report nobody reads.

You don’t have to cancel it. Just know which one it is.
